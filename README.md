# Project overview
The goal of this project is to explore and analyze the World Happiness Report 2024 dataset to uncover patterns and insights about global well-being. The dataset contains key indicators such as life satisfaction, GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom, generosity, and perceptions of corruption across countries and years.

This project involves cleaning and transforming the data to handle missing values and ensure consistency, making it ready for analysis. By filling in missing numeric values with median values and sorting the data by year, we ensure that the dataset is complete and easy to work with.

Using this cleaned dataset, the project then visualizes trends and comparisons across countries and regions. Charts highlight the happiest countries, the impact of socio-economic factors on life satisfaction, and year-over-year changes, making it easier for users to understand global happiness trends at a glance.

Ultimately, this project demonstrates how data analysis and visualization can turn raw datasets into actionable insights. It provides an interactive and informative experience for anyone interested in understanding the factors that influence happiness around the world.

🌍 1. Global Overview

The dataset contains data from 165 countries, covering variables such as GDP, Life Expectancy, Freedom, Generosity, and Social Support.

The average Life Ladder (happiness index) across all countries is approximately 5.48, showing that global happiness remains moderate with strong variation between regions.

💰 2. Economic Indicators

GDP remains one of the strongest predictors of happiness.

The Top 5 countries by GDP show a consistent upward trend over the years, indicating stable economic growth and higher living standards.

In contrast, the Bottom 5 countries by GDP reveal lower and more fluctuating values, suggesting potential economic instability or limited resources.

This highlights that while economic prosperity contributes to happiness, it is not the only factor, as some countries with moderate GDP still report above-average happiness.

🧬 3. Health and Well-being

Life Expectancy shows a clear correlation with happiness and GDP.

Countries such as Japan, Switzerland, and Iceland rank highest in life expectancy, reflecting strong healthcare systems and quality of life.

Shorter life expectancy often coincides with lower happiness and economic scores.

 🧠 4. Social and Emotional Factors

Positive Affect and Social Support metrics vary widely by region.

Countries with strong community networks tend to report higher happiness scores even if their GDP is lower, showing that emotional and social well-being significantly contribute to life satisfaction.

📈 5. Overall Observations

Economic, social, and health indicators are interconnected.

While GDP and Life Expectancy have direct correlations with happiness, social support and freedom play a key role in differentiating countries with similar economic performance.

Developing nations show improving trends over time, suggesting global progress in well-being and life quality.
